Yes. **That's another major problem.** And on the screenshots, I can see it clearly.

The page isn't just "a little crowded." The design system is making **everything look equally important**.

### The hierarchy problem

Right now you have roughly this visual weight everywhere:

**Eyebrow**
**Heading**
**Paragraph**
**Card**
**Another heading**
**Another paragraph**
**Another card**
**Another divider**

Then another section starts immediately.

So the eye never gets a strong:

> **THIS is the important thing.**

The dashed borders and patterned background make it worse because **every block is boxed and separated**, which creates visual noise.

Not 15 equally important sections.

### 01 — Hero

Huge breathing room.

> **Automate The**
> **Busy Work**

One sentence.

One CTA.

One compelling visual.

Nothing else competing with it.

# Your Team Shouldn't Spend Its Day Doing This.

Then **3 big examples**, not 8 tiny cards.

> Copying information between spreadsheets.

> Building the same reports every week.

> Formatting documents that already have a template.

**We remove the work around the work.**

That's a section people will actually remember.

# We Find It. We Fix It. We Automate It.

We build the systems that handle the rest.

This is substantially cleaner than having six tiny "Data Entry / Approvals / Communication" cards.

# And visually, I'd make one huge change

The current page is built around **boxes**.

Everything is a box.

```text
┌───────────────┐
│ section       │
└───────────────┘
┌───────────────┐
│ section       │
└───────────────┘
┌───────────────┐
│ section       │
└───────────────┘
```

That's why it feels cramped even when there is technically empty space.

I'd let some sections **breathe into the background**.

Use the Mizu grid/pattern as a subtle structural element, but don't put a visible border around every thought.

Think:

**large open area → statement → visual → large open area**

rather than:

**box → box → box → box → box**

## There is also a hierarchy rule I'd establish

### Level 1

The things we absolutely need the visitor to remember.

**AUTOMATE THE BUSY WORK**

### Level 2

The major ideas.

**Where Your Team Loses Time**
**How We Fix It**
**Before & After**

### Level 3

Supporting details.

Paragraphs, labels, metadata.

### Level 4

Tiny information.

Eyebrows, captions, secondary links.

Right now your site sometimes makes a paragraph and a major headline feel almost equally important.

That's the "no hierarchy" feeling you're noticing.

And I would **not rebuild the site from scratch**.

The Mizu structure is usable.

We should keep the good bones and progressively replace the generic blocks.

Your new homepage should feel less like:
